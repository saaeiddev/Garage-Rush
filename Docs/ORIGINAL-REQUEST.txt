### 1. PRODUCT AND SCOPE

Create a photorealistic single-player automotive workshop game. The player owns a professional garage, accepts customer jobs, diagnoses vehicle problems, physically services components, buys replacement parts, tunes vehicles, and verifies repairs on a closed test track.

Deliver a polished first release with:

- One fully explorable, detailed garage.
- Three visually distinct, fully modeled vehicles.
- One closed test facility.
- At least eight playable repair orders.
- Career progression, an inventory, a parts shop, persistent saves, and a sandbox mode.
- Approximately 30–60 minutes of varied initial gameplay, with repeatable jobs afterward.

Treat the garage, cars, mechanical interaction, and driving as the visual and gameplay priorities. Allocate time to finish and polish this defined scope.

### 3. VISUAL QUALITY: THE MOST IMPORTANT REQUIREMENT

The garage, cars, interiors, engines, and serviceable parts must look highly realistic. Aim for the visual quality of a premium automotive visualization and a modern PC simulation game.

Use real-world scale and reference-based modeling. Maintain accurate proportions, coherent construction, correct normals, appropriate UVs, and consistent texture density.

For Unreal rendering:

- Use Nanite for suitable assets supported by the selected engine version.
- Use Lumen global illumination and reflections where the performance budget allows.
- Use Virtual Shadow Maps or an appropriate tested shadow configuration.
- Provide optional hardware ray tracing for supported GPUs.
- Provide a validated lower-cost lighting/reflection configuration.
- Use TSR; add compatible DLSS only if its plugin is available, redistributable, and tested.
- Use controlled exposure and restrained cinematic color grading.
- Keep motion blur, film grain, depth of field, and camera shake individually adjustable.

Use high-quality PBR materials:

- Layered automotive paint with believable clear coat.
- Brushed and machined metal, cast aluminum, steel, chrome, rubber, leather, fabric, glass, plastic, concrete, and painted surfaces.
- Fine roughness variation, normal detail, subtle scratches, dirt, oil residue, and appropriate edge wear.
- Physically plausible reflections and material response.

Allocate texture resolution according to visibility and memory. Use 4K textures on key close-up surfaces and 2K or lower where appropriate. Use higher resolutions only when they produce a visible benefit within the measured budget.

Inspect the final packaged game at normal viewing distance and in close-ups. Correct missing materials, faceting, stretched textures, floating parts, visible gaps, exaggerated bloom, unstable reflections, and unreadable details.

Build the visible world with finished production assets. Temporary blockouts must be replaced before delivery.

### 7. REPAIR AND INTERACTION SYSTEM

Use first-person movement in the garage. When working on a part, offer a smooth contextual inspection camera, focus controls, and optional labels.

The interaction flow must support:

- Looking at and selecting the actual visible component.
- Inspecting wear and condition.
- Choosing and using the appropriate tool.
- Removing required fasteners or prerequisites.
- Removing the component.
- Buying or selecting a compatible replacement.
- Installing and securing it.
- Performing the appropriate follow-up test.

Model key visible fastening operations with believable positions, rotation, and tool feedback. Make a dependency system enforce access: for example, remove a wheel before servicing its brake assembly.

Prevent duplicate inventory items, impossible removals, incompatible installations, repeated rewards, and charging for failed operations. Inventory, money, vehicle state, and task completion must update coherently.

Give clear feedback when an action is unavailable. Provide an optional assisted mode with highlights, tool suggestions, and the next valid step. Make labels and guidance disableable.

Use a data-driven component model with stable IDs, health, compatibility, dependencies, installation state, and relevant diagnostic properties.
